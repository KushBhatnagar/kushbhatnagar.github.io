**TL;DR:** For five weeks, every LinkedIn post I published was drafted in [Sahayak](/build-log/sahayak-ai-linkedin-drafts-not-finished-posts/), the tool I built. Most
posts got modest reach, two broke out, and I learned that impressions, reactions and trust are three
different things. Not a flop, but a lot of learning.

## The problem

Indian professionals want to build a personal brand, but staying consistent is hard. I'm one of them. So
before launching Sahayak, I ran a stealth month: I used Sahayak for every post, without telling anyone, to
see whether it actually helps me stay consistent.

## What I did

- **15 posts between 27 September and 1 November** (14 during the stealth month, plus the launch post), across five frameworks: Concept Breakdown, Hot Takes,
  Build in Public, Story Post, and Insights & Learnings.
- **Every draft came from Sahayak, and I edited every one.** When the context is ready, a draft takes me
  around 1.5–2 minutes. Hot Takes and Insights & Learnings take longer, because I have to do the research first.
- **I tracked everything** in a spreadsheet: impressions, reactions, comments, profile views and
  followers.

## The one thing

**Two posts brought most of the reach, and I couldn't have predicted which ones.**

| | Anthropic and Git (Hot Take) | [Job Sherpa](/build-log/job-sherpa-open-source-job-search-assistant/) (Build in Public) |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | 13,614 | 30,949 |
| Reactions | 9 | 71 |
| Saves | 2 | 108 |
| Sends | 1 | 24 |
| Profile views | 19 | 166 |
| Followers gained | 3 | 27 |

Together they made up about 85% of my 52,367 impressions.
The other 13 posts mostly reached a few hundred people.

That's why consistency matters to me now. Every post is a chance. I can't pick the winner in advance, but
posting regularly gives me more chances.

The second lesson is in the table: **reach is not trust.** Both posts reached people far outside my network
(98% out of network). The Anthropic post got people to look. Job Sherpa got people to save it, send it and
follow me. People react to a post which has some pain or fear attached, like job loss or Anthropic
replacing something.

And I learned why engagement rate misled me. It divides by impressions, so where impressions were low the
engagement rate was high, but the number of reactions was not. Where impressions were high, the engagement
rate was low, but reactions were high. People see a post without necessarily reacting; that's normal on
LinkedIn. So I count high impressions as a win, to some extent, and I watch what people *do*: profile views,
saves and follows.

Overall, followers grew from 998 to 1,123. 41 of them came in the first week, after my first three posts.

## What's still broken / next

- **I missed two targets.** My engagement rate was below the 3% I aimed for, and I logged only 8 unique
  commenters against a goal of 40–50. Most comments were appreciation, or questions about how the tool would
  help. One was a question about evals, which I answered with real numbers.
- **The [reveal](/build-log/sahayak-ai-linkedin-drafts-not-finished-posts/) brought 2 sign-ups so far.** There's a lot of activity on the home page, but not much beyond
  that. I have a new strategy to reach more people, and that's next.
- **I'll keep posting and keep tracking,** now watching saves and profile views, not just engagement rate.
